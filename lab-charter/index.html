Group Charter

Overview

The Health Sensing and Microsystems Group is a community. We want to create a culture that allows everyone to excel both professionally and personally, and that is inclusive and embraces diversity. This culture is created by all of us, and so all members of the group are expected to show respect and courtesy to others at all times.

This charter supplements, but does not take priority over, School of Electrical and Electronic Engineering, College or University-level policies.

Inclusion and diversity

We value an inclusive and diverse environment to support each individual's development and research, and to promote robust decision making and high-quality research. All lab members are dedicated to a harassment-free experience for everyone, regardless of gender identity and expression, sexual orientation, disability, physical appearance, body size, race, age and/or religion. We do not tolerate harassment by and/or of members of our lab in any form.

Be kind to others, and do not insult or put down other lab members. We acknowledge that we will grow as a team and learn from each other through positive, constructive criticism.

More detail can be found in UCD's Dignity and Respect Policy (covering the Bullying and Harassment Policy and the Sexual Misconduct Policy).

If you experience harassment, bullying, discrimination or other inappropriate behaviour, you can contact Marco at any time. You do not have to raise it with Marco first, and UCD provides several dedicated, confidential routes if you would rather not:

  • UCD Report and Support tool: an anonymous online reporting option for students, staff and visitors
  • UCD Dignity and Respect Support Advisers: a trained team offering confidential, non-judgemental listening and guidance on informal and formal options
  • Panel of Dignity and Respect Support Colleagues: trained staff and faculty volunteers based across the University; to ensure impartiality, choose someone you don't already know
  • UCD Student Advisory Service: for students, each programme has a dedicated Student Adviser who can support with personal, social and emotional issues and signpost to the right policy or service
  • UCD Employee Assistance Service: for staff, a free, confidential, 24/7 service covering dignity and respect issues as well as broader personal or work-related concerns

Mental health

There is increasing evidence that certain attributes of research may challenge your mental health. Specific factors driving this include:

  • High expectations and pressure to perform
  • Long hours and high workload
  • Low pay and quality-of-life issues
  • Feelings of isolation in your research, since everyone has their own topic and it can often feel as if you are working alone
  • Uncertainty in your research; in research of all kinds, it is not just the outcomes that are uncertain, but the questions themselves
  • Uncertainty in your career
  • So-called "negative results", where at some point certain questions prove more challenging to answer than anticipated, or you feel you have spent days, months or years toiling with little to show
  • Burnout, meaning the feeling that you need to work endless hours to make up for the above, and the exhaustion that follows

Researchers at all career stages, including Master's and PhD students, postdoctoral researchers and academic staff, come across most of these issues at some level. I strongly encourage everyone in the group to take an active and pre-emptive approach to maintaining their mental health. One of my responsibilities is making sure you have the time and resources needed for successful research. If anything is placing undue stress on you, or preventing you from performing at your potential, let Marco know. Sharing these issues can reduce stress and help others accommodate your needs, and we may be able to assist directly.

Please also familiarise yourself with the mental health and wellbeing resources available through UCD. Staff can use the UCD Employee Assistance Service (a free, confidential, 24/7 service) and the UCD Wellbeing Hub. Students can access the UCD Student Counselling Service and their Student Adviser.

Work-life balance

We believe that maintaining a healthy work-life balance is essential. The scientific endeavour can be frustrating and challenging, with failures along the way. To help us recharge and maintain the energy required to achieve our long-term vision, it is important to take time out for the other things we enjoy, whether that is family, friends, music, sport, cooking or any other hobby. Maintaining a good work-life balance ensures that, in the long term, you are both happy and productive.

Flexible working and annual leave

We work flexibly, and independently:

  • Lab members are trusted to organise their own working pattern. There is no fixed hours convention; instead, we expect members to work independently and to deliver against the research output and milestones agreed with Marco. What matters is the quality and progress of the work, not hours logged.
  • The scheduling of hours members choose to work is up to them, with the caveat that some facilities may only be accessible during certain hours for health and safety reasons.
  • It is important to respect the time and efforts of others, and to avoid situations in which your own working pattern negatively impacts someone else who does not have the same flexibility.
  • It is also important to respect the contribution of funders. Where there are key deadlines, work should be prioritised accordingly.
  • Hybrid or remote working is supported where compatible with research goals, laboratory and facility access, health and safety, and visa or residency requirements. Staff should follow UCD's policies on flexible and remote working. Postgraduate research students should agree any working-away arrangements with Marco in advance and consult UCD's terms for graduate research students.
  • Group meetings are held weekly to check in on lab safety, shared calendar items and general group matters. All lab members are expected to attend, in person or virtually where an agreed working-away arrangement applies.

Annual leave:

  • Arrange annual leave or holidays in accordance with UCD and School requirements, and with regard to key deadlines and group commitments. Postgraduate research students should agree holiday dates with Marco. As a courtesy, note any absences in the group's shared calendar so others know when you will be away.
  • Take your holidays, respect weekends (or take time in lieu), and establish a sustainable work-life balance. If you struggle to establish this balance, raise it with Marco early.

Communication and meetings

We prepare for one-to-one meetings. Please come ready to discuss:

  • Your progress against previously agreed action items
  • Questions or problems you need help with
  • Your thoughts and plans, however tentative: an experiment or simulation you are considering, a paper you might write, a potential collaboration, an internship, some teaching you would like to do, or your next career step. I want to hear about it; I may have useful advice to share.

Much of our work is coordinated via Teams. You can ask questions one-to-one or raise them with the whole group. This is a less formal mode of interaction for quick questions and sharing results, and does not require follow-up notes. I may read messages without replying immediately; this usually means I am thinking about the problem, not that I am uninterested.

In our regular group meetings:

  • Members are expected to attend unless engaged in other academic business (for example, a conference, training or a lecture)
  • Members are expected to show respect for others by paying attention during presentations and discussion, including putting phones away
  • Members are welcome to send messages over the weekend or late at night, but no one is required to reply outside their own working hours

Manuscript writing

Embrace disseminating your work; it is part of being a good researcher.

Papers and chapters take time to write, and the first few drafts are always rough. Start writing as early as possible and ask for regular feedback; this will help you write better papers. It may take several rounds of drafting and comments to produce a paper that stands a good chance of being accepted.

Please give at least a week's notice when asking for feedback. Where possible, plan and agree deadlines for each draft you will need to produce. Leaving writing to the last minute asks your co-authors to give up their free time to help make your paper as good as it can be; we would much rather help you during normal working hours.

Publication targets and timing will be agreed individually, depending on the project and workload, rather than fixed for everyone. As a general guide, submitting a conference paper early in the PhD and considering whether thesis chapters could stand alone as journal papers are both worth discussing with Marco as the project develops. Publications often cluster near the end of a PhD, so do not be discouraged if they are not ready until the final year.

We provide supplementary materials with our papers wherever possible. The group is very much a supporter for open source and open data, and we encourage you to share your code and data with the community. We will help you do this in a way that is compatible with journal and conference policies.

Writing can be lengthy and mentally taxing, but it matters. Try to write something towards your next paper, thesis or grant for a short period most days.

Authorship

Collaborate often, and always acknowledge the work of others.

Authorship on any manuscript or presentation will be openly discussed with Marco, and we aim to be inclusive of everyone who has made a significant contribution to the work. A significant contribution can include, but is not limited to, developing ideas, or collecting, analysing or interpreting data. Even work not ultimately included in the paper should be acknowledged; a negative result from someone else may have been crucial in guiding the path to a positive one.

We base authorship on the NIH criteria, all of which must be met:

We aim for open dialogue about authorship, ideally checking in several times over the lifetime of a study. Discuss with Marco who, outside your immediate collaborators, might be an author before approaching them.

You must request permission before submitting a paper for peer review, and confirm that all authors are content for the paper to be submitted, including after re-review.

Author order will typically follow the conventions of the field: the lead author normally leads the writing, and other up-front positions reflect approximate contribution. Later author positions are often held by supervisors. Where there is disagreement, Marco will facilitate discussion to help resolve it.

Failure

Failure is an inevitable result of trying innovative things at the cutting edge of science and technology. We encourage a culture where failure is embraced as a way of learning what doesn't work, so we can quickly solve problems or refocus our efforts. If you are not failing from time to time, you are probably not being ambitious enough. Failing well means recovering quickly, learning what you can from it, and thinking creatively about what to do next.

Mentoring and personal development

Marco's aim as group leader is to be a mentor to all members, both in research and in personal and professional development.

We will have continuous review meetings to discuss research goals, plan for the term ahead, and set personal and professional development goals.

In return, I hope you will mentor each other, particularly junior and visiting members such as undergraduate project students. Mentoring is one of the key aspects of a thriving research culture, and all members should engage with it.

What you can expect of Marco:

  • To be invested in your success and wellbeing, and to do my best to help you become an excellent researcher
  • To provide honest, constructive feedback on your work
  • To review your development plans, help you set goals, and provide honest, thoughtful career advice
  • To work with the group to set ambitious but reasonable research goals
  • To work to bring in funding to keep research going
  • To publicise your research to promote it and encourage new collaborations and opportunities
  • To consult the group before recruiting new members
  • To review drafts within a week of receiving them, though revisions may take longer
  • To support you to attend at least one conference per year
  • To invite you to work on exciting side projects; feel free to say no, but please do not commit if you do not have the time
  • To include you in papers where your contribution merits it
  • To create and support an inclusive and diverse lab culture

What Marco expects of you:

  • To take ownership of your research project, and strive to achieve your goals and produce high-quality results
  • To feel free to propose new project or proposal ideas; I am happy to discuss them, but will ask you to take ownership of the first few drafts
  • To embrace and celebrate failure; research at the cutting edge is highly uncertain and will involve failures along the way
  • To listen to the advice I give. You do not always need to follow it, but if you consistently ignore it, it becomes difficult for me to help you
  • To support, mentor and collaborate with others whenever you can
  • To work with other lab members to resolve problems or conflicts, and contribute to the general upkeep of the lab, including administrative duties such as maintaining lab computing resources and occasionally helping to keep the lab space tidy
  • To maintain good lines of communication with Marco, so we can celebrate results but also quickly identify problems
  • To investigate and discuss appropriate funding opportunities
  • By the time of your viva, aim to be the most knowledgeable person in the room on your topic
  • To communicate your career goals to Marco as they develop
