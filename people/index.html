People

We are an interdisciplinary team working across circuits, materials, firmware and data science.

Our group operates according to a Charter, which sets out how we work together: our values on inclusion and respect, expectations around flexible working and communication, and what members can expect from Marco as group lead and from each other. New and prospective members are encouraged to read it before joining.

Principal Investigator

Marco Vinicio Alban-Paccha, MEng, PhD, FHEA, MIEEE

BDIC Lecturer / Assistant Professor in Electronic Engineering, Principal Investigator

Systems co-design for wearable and implantable medical devices

Marco is a Lecturer/Assistant Professor at the School of Electronic Engineering in University College Dublin. His work builds the supporting technologies that let wearable and implantable bioelectronic devices operate reliably outside the lab, spanning analogue front ends, embedded and mixed-signal systems, low-power wireless backends, firmware stacks and data governance. He approaches sensing, computation, communications and power as a single co-designed system with a focus on autonomy, data integrity, latency and clinical translation.

Previously, Marco was a Postdoctoral Research Associate (2022–2025) at the University of Cambridge as part of the ADVANTAGE consortium for visceral pain, while also being an Associate Lecturer at the Open University, and a Postdoctoral By-Fellow at Churchill College. Born in Quito, Ecuador, he earned a Mechatronics Engineering degree at the University of the Armed Forces (2013), an MEng in Micro/Nano Systems at Korea University (2018, GKS scholar), and a PhD in Electrical Engineering at KAIST (2022).

Team

Roman Percht, MEng

Doctoral Researcher

Low-power embedded systems for translational biomedical applications

Roman's research focuses on the design and integration of low-power embedded systems for translational biomedical applications, spanning sensing, signal processing, wireless communication, and secure data handling. He is particularly interested in how hardware, firmware, and data pipelines can be co-designed to enable reliable, energy-efficient, and clinically relevant bioelectronic systems.

He previously completed a Master’s degree in Biomedical Engineering at University College Dublin (2024), where he developed a tactile sensing system integrated into a 3D-printed prosthetic hand for sensory feedback. During his studies, he gained industry experience at FIRE1, contributing to hardware and firmware development as well as regulatory processes for an implantable RF sensing device. Roman also holds a Bachelor’s degree in Medical Device Technology from the University of Applied Sciences Upper Austria (2022), where his work included the development of low-cost EMG electrodes for control of upper limb prosthetics.

For prospective members

The group welcomes people with backgrounds in electrical and electronic engineering, biomedical engineering, computer engineering, or related fields. Members will work on tightly coupled hardware and software problems, gaining experience across analogue electronics, embedded systems, wireless communications, firmware development, and data-centric system design.

Projects are hands-on and system-oriented, often involving custom electronics, low-power firmware stacks, wireless backends, and the analysis of real-world physiological and clinical datasets. Students are encouraged to engage with clinical collaborators and to consider how engineering choices affect usability, safety, privacy, and translational potential.

The training environment emphasises strong engineering fundamentals, independence, and cross-disciplinary thinking. Graduates of the group are prepared for careers in academia, medtech, neurotechnology, and advanced healthcare engineering.

We welcome enquiries from prospective PhD students and postdoctoral researchers interested in wearable and implantable bioelectronics, ultra-low-power analogue front ends, neural data acquisition, and organic electrochemical transistors (OECTs). Beyond any advertised posts, we support strong candidates in applying for external scholarships and fellowships; the main schemes are listed below.

We will soon be recruiting research assistants and postdoctoral researchers; roles will be announced via the News section and shared on LinkedIn/X.

Funding and scholarships

Doctoral scholarships

UCD and Ireland-wide

  • Government of Ireland Postgraduate Scholarship (IRC)Covers stipend and fees for PhD study at any Irish university. Open to all nationalities.
  • Government of Ireland International Education ScholarshipHEA-run scheme selecting 60 scholars annually across Ireland. Open to postgraduate applicants holding an offer.
  • UCD Graduate Studies scholarships and fundingCentral listing of UCD-funded and UCD-hosted PhD schemes.
  • UCD Global scholarships for international studentsFilterable by country; several schemes cover STEM postgraduate study.

Nationality-specific schemes

  • UCD China Scholarship SchemeJoint UCD / China Scholarship Council funding for Chinese doctoral candidates. Opens every January.
  • Bolashaq Scholarship ProgrammeFully funded scheme for Kazakh students at PhD level.
  • COLFUTUROScholarship-loan programme for Colombian postgraduate students.
  • FIDERH — Banco de MéxicoPostgraduate funding for Mexican students.
  • FUNEDPostgraduate scholarships and grants for high-achieving Mexican students.

Marshall, Fulbright and other national schemes are also worth checking depending on your country of origin; the UCD Global scholarships list can be filtered by country.

Postdoctoral fellowships

Postdoctoral fellowships

  • Government of Ireland Postdoctoral Fellowship (IRC)Two-year fellowship for early-career researchers to conduct independent research at an Irish institution.
  • Marie Skłodowska-Curie Postdoctoral FellowshipsEU funding for researchers of any nationality to work in Ireland.
  • Research Ireland Frontiers for the FutureFormerly the SFI scheme of the same name. A route toward independent funding for researchers with a track record; rolling deadline, four to five years.
  • Research Ireland Enterprise FellowshipsSuccessor to the SFI Industry Fellowship Programme. Supports researcher placements and partnerships with industry; check the site for the next call.
  • Newton International FellowshipsRoyal Society scheme for researchers with under seven years' postdoctoral experience to work in the UK or Ireland.
  • 1851 Research FellowshipsFor early-career scientists and engineers of exceptional promise pursuing a self-directed project.

Beyond the schemes listed here, ECR Central and Research Ireland list broader funding opportunities, and we are always glad to support applications to international schemes (MSCA, Erasmus and similar) that name Marco as supervisor. If you are interested in joining the group, get in touch with a short note on your background and research interests, along with which of the above schemes you may be eligible for.
